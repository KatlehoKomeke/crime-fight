# ADR 0006 — Bounties: a provider-agnostic ledger, dual control, and Paystack behind an interface

- **Status:** Accepted (November 2026). Payments stay `disabled` in production until [the go-live gate](#the-go-live-gate) below is cleared; the board is read-only until then.

## Goal

A member who suffered a crime posts a reward (R200–R50 000) for a verifiable outcome: a stolen vehicle or item recovered through SAPS, a missing person found safe, or information handed to SAPS. They pay the reward in up front, CIVIC/ONE holds it, and it is paid to the claimant only after an administrator has checked the claim, the poster has confirmed it or had the chance to object, and an administrator has verified it after a cooling-off period; otherwise it goes back to the poster. Money must be impossible to lose, double-pay or move without a record; the payment provider must be swappable, because whether CIVIC/ONE may hold other people's money at all is a legal question that isn't settled; and nothing may move real money until it is.

## Product rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Paid in up front and held.** A bounty isn't listed until its deposit settles; the board shows only funded bounties. Nothing is spent, lent or invested. | An unfunded promise is the fraud the feature exists to prevent. A claimant can see the money exists before they do anything. |
| **Nobody is named.** Bounty text can't contain an `@mention`; a bounty describes a vehicle, an item, a missing person or a case, never a suspect. ID and card numbers are refused outright, and a missing-person bounty can't carry a street address. Claims are private to the claimant and admins, and to the poster once an admin approves one; the public sees the count and an activity trail that says a claim came in, never from whom. | A reward for a person is a vigilante bounty. CIVIC/ONE pays for outcomes SAPS can confirm. The deterministic rules fail fast with a message the form shows; judgement calls go through reports and review. |
| **Each side sees only its own side.** The activity trail shows a viewer the public events plus the ones addressed to or done by them; only admins see all of it. The poster's note when confirming a claim goes to the reviewer and never to the claimant; who reported a bounty is in `bounty_reports` for admins and never on the poster's trail; a withdrawn claim leaves the poster's view; an admin's note is labelled with who will read it. Claims render as plain text, with no tappable links or mentions. | A claim reaches someone who is known to be holding money, and a report can come from the person a bounty describes. Nothing in those channels should hand one party a way to reach, pressure or phish the other. |
| **One claim per person per bounty.** A claim can be withdrawn, but not sent again on the same bounty. | Withdrawing and re-claiming would keep a bounty from ever being cancelled or expiring, since a live claim blocks both. |
| **Property comes back through SAPS.** A claim attests the claimant wasn't involved and didn't buy the item back. | Buying back stolen goods is itself an offence (receiving stolen property) and funds the next theft. |
| **Information goes to SAPS first.** The evidence is the station and officer spoken to, never the information itself, and the cooling-off is seven days instead of three. | Withholding information until paid can be a crime; CIVIC/ONE must never be where a tip-off is traded. An arrest or charge takes longer to confirm than a returned bakkie. |
| **CIVIC/ONE checks a claim before the poster sees it; the poster then confirms or objects; CIVIC/ONE has the final say.** A new claim goes to the admin queue and to every admin's notifications; the poster is told only that a claim came in. An admin approves it (with a note both sides see) or rejects it, and the poster never sees a rejected one. Once approved, the poster sees the claim and who sent it and has 7 days to confirm or object with a reason for the reviewer; silence counts as no objection. The kind's cooling-off (72 h for property and missing persons, 7 days for information) runs from approval. After it, an admin verifies and the transfer is sent; paying over an objection is recorded as an `ADMIN_OVERRIDE`. | A poster who reads a claim first can use what it says (where the car is, which station has the information) and then refuse it; checking first takes that away, and keeps a missing person's whereabouts with CIVIC/ONE. Poster and claimant colluding is still covered: two people, a delay and a note on every release, and the admin's `ReleasePreflight` (claimant newer than the bounty, no other activity, prior paid pairings with this poster, payout name mismatch, account age) in front of the payment. |
| **A claim needs a bank account.** A claim can only be sent with an `ACTIVE` payout account; the 72-hour hold on a new account still applies at payout, and fits inside the time a claim takes to decide. | A claim is a claim on money: it should say where the money goes before anyone spends time on it, and an account added at the last minute is the mule move the hold exists to stop. |
| **Claims need an established account.** An account must be 72 hours old before it can claim (`BOUNTY_CLAIM_MIN_ACCOUNT_AGE_HOURS`, default 72). | Whoever has the property can't open an account the day the bounty appears and claim it. With the payout-account hold and the cooling-off, about a week stands between a new identity and any money. |
| **One bank account per person, held 72 h.** The account number is never stored: an HMAC fingerprint ties one account to one person for good (replaced or not; one left behind by a deleted account can be linked afresh, and starts the hold again), a new account can't receive money for 72 hours, and a payout goes only to the claimant's own `ACTIVE` account. A retried payout is held to the same rule and records a fresh preflight for the account actually paid. | Changing the account minutes before a payout is the classic mule move, and "let the first payout bounce, then swap the account" is the same move one step later; the fingerprint stops one account collecting under many names without CIVIC/ONE holding account numbers. |
| **Full refund if nothing comes of it.** The poster can cancel while no claim is live; expiry, a declined review and an admin cancellation refund; a refund goes only to the original payment. With a live claim the poster can only ask CIVIC/ONE to cancel. A refund the provider fails stays in the admin queue and in the poster's notifications, blocks the poster's account deletion, and is sent again by an admin (`retryBountyRefund`). | The poster never loses by posting; the only money out is to a verified claimant. Refund-to-source means there is no balance to withdraw, so CIVIC/ONE is never a wallet. |
| **Missing-person bounties are reviewed first and capped at 30 days.** A funded one lands in `PENDING_REVIEW`; an admin approves it onto the board or declines it with a refund. The SAPS case number is required and public (a sighting needs it to call 10111); how the poster knows the person is seen only by the poster and admins. Page titles, previews and structured data say "missing person", never who. One declined, or cancelled before review, was never listed and never becomes public; its description is removed seven days after it closes. | A bounty can endanger the person it describes, including someone who left a violent home. A short clock and a human before anything is public, and nothing public afterwards that wasn't public before. |
| **Limits.** R200–R50 000 per bounty in whole rands; R100 000 held per poster, counting bounties not paid for yet; 3 open bounties per poster (unpaid ones count); 5 claims a day; one claim per person per bounty; one winning claim per bounty. The caps are checked under a lock on the poster. | Caps bound what any one fraud or chargeback can cost, so they count what could be paid for a moment later. Every number lives in `packages/core/src/bounty.ts` and the API, the app and the terms import it, so changing one is a deploy, not a rewrite. |
| **Frozen on chargebacks; reports go to review.** A card dispute freezes the bounty at once (the poster is told) and nothing moves while the bank decides. A dispute the cardholder wins (`merchant-accepted`, `auto-accepted`) closes the bounty by itself: the bank already made the refund, so it is booked as refunded and no second one is sent. Any other outcome stays on hold until an admin, with the Paystack dashboard in front of them, releases it; releasing books the disputed money as held again. A report puts the bounty in the admin queue, where it can be frozen, cancelled and refunded, and taken down. | While the bank decides whether the deposit was legitimate, paying it out would be paying with money we may have to give back; and a webhook that says "resolved" can't be trusted to say who won. |
| **Admin actions are never attributed by name, and never on an admin's own bounty.** Events by an admin show as "CIVIC/ONE"; the `actor_id` stays in the row for audit. No admin action (decide, approve, decline, cancel, freeze, release, retry) runs on a bounty the admin posted or claimed. | A reviewer who can be identified can be pressured, and the second pair of eyes in dual control has to belong to someone else. |
| **Records outlive accounts; pages don't.** Deleting an account is blocked while money is held for or owed to it; afterwards the bounties, claims, ledger rows and events keep going with `null` people, and the person's closed bounties come off everyone's screen but CIVIC/ONE's and their claimants'. Claim text is removed a year after a bounty closes. | Financial records have to survive the person who made them; what they wrote doesn't have to stay public. |
| **Taken down means taken down.** An admin cancelling a bounty can take its page down as well; a closed bounty is public only if it was ever listed and was not taken down. | A shared link should still explain itself after an ordinary cancellation, and should show nothing after a takedown. |

## Money model

Money is tracked twice on purpose: every movement is a `ledger_entries` row (`DEPOSIT` · `RELEASE` · `REFUND` · `REVERSAL` · `CHARGEBACK` · `CHARGEBACK_REVERSED`, each `PENDING` → `SETTLED` | `FAILED` | `MISMATCH`), and the bounty carries running totals under `CHECK (released_cents + refunded_cents <= deposited_cents)`, so what goes out can never exceed what came in, whatever the code does.

- **Every transition is `UPDATE … WHERE status = <prior> RETURNING`**, inside one transaction with its ledger and `bounty_events` rows. Two requests can't both move the same money: the second finds no row and gets "This bounty changed while you were looking at it."
- **Provider calls only after commit.** Checkout, transfer and refund are asked for only once the transaction recording the intent has committed, and their results are applied by idempotent functions (`settleDeposit`, `applyTransfer`, `applyRefund`) keyed on *our* reference. The return page, the webhook and the hourly sweep all run the same functions. A crash between commit and provider leaves a `PENDING` row that the sweep re-drives; a transfer is fetched by reference before it is sent again, so a retry never pays twice.
- **References are ours and deterministic:** `dep-<bounty>-<n>`, `rel-<claim>-<n>`, `ref-<bounty>-<n>`, plus `rev-…` and `cbk-…` for what the bank does to us. The attempt number means a replaced checkout or a retried payout is a new row, never a changed one. `reference` and `idempotency_key` (`release:<claim>:<n>` and so on) are both unique.
- **Partial unique indexes promise what code paths can't:** one live deposit per bounty (`ledger_one_live_deposit_idx`), one release per bounty (`ledger_one_release_idx`, over `PENDING` and `SETTLED`), one approved claim per bounty (`bounty_claims_one_winner_idx`: `APPROVED` / `CONFIRMED` / `DISPUTED` / `VERIFIED` / `PAID`), one live claim per person per bounty, one `ACTIVE` payout account per person and one person per account fingerprint.
- **Money counts only when the provider says so, and only `success` counts.** A `charge.success` webhook is never credited from its body: the API re-verifies the reference with the provider. Paystack's `abandoned` is a checkout the customer hasn't finished and the page stays payable, so it is `PENDING`, not a failure; only `failed` and `reversed` are final, and only the bounty's own 24-hour window ends a checkout. A deposit whose amount or currency differs from what was asked is recorded as `MISMATCH` for an admin and the bounty stays unfunded.
- **A payment on a checkout already written off is still money.** A written-off deposit row (replaced by "Pay again", timed out, deleted) that the provider later reports paid is taken in: it funds the bounty if that is still unpaid (closing any newer checkout), and otherwise it is returned in full, whether the bounty closed or was funded by the other checkout. A second payment keeps its written-off row (one live deposit per bounty is a database rule), dated, with its refund beside it.
- **Refunds name their deposit.** A refund's key is `refund:<bounty>:<deposit>:<n>`, so the provider is asked to refund that transaction and no other, and a refund never depends on finding "the settled deposit". Paystack's refund and dispute events carry the transaction's *reference*, which is ours, and are matched on it. A refund Paystack accepts as `pending` settles on `refund.processed`, or, if that event never comes, when the sweep asks `GET /refund/:id`. A refund the provider fails stays owed: the bounty is in the admin queue whatever its status, the poster is told, and `retryBountyRefund` queues attempt `n+1`.
- **Transfers.** A transfer the provider refuses outright (a 4xx that isn't "already exists": no balance, dead recipient) and one it reverses before it ever settled are both a failed payout: the row fails, the claimant is told, and an admin retries once the cause is fixed. A reversal after settling is the `REVERSAL` row described under the state machine. A refusal the sweep can't tell from an outage (a timeout, a 5xx) is left `PENDING` for the next hour.
- **`payment_events` dedupes webhooks** on `(provider, event_key)`. Paystack has no event id, so the key is the event name plus the object's id, or a hash of the body. The row is inserted before the event is applied and its `outcome` written after, so a redelivery of an applied event is a no-op; a redelivery of one that failed to apply (the row still says `RECEIVED`, because the apply threw and the webhook answered 500) is the retry, which is safe because every apply step is idempotent on the ledger.
- **Ledger rows are immutable.** A trigger refuses `DELETE` and any change to the amount, type, bounty, reference, idempotency key or currency; a row may only settle or fail. Reversals and chargebacks are new rows.
- **The hourly sweep** (`sweep()` in `apps/api/src/data/bounties.ts`) abandons `PENDING_PAYMENT` bounties older than 24 h, expires and refunds `OPEN` bounties past their date that have no live claim, writes the "expires in 3 days" event, marks an approved claim the poster hasn't answered in 7 days as `CONFIRMED` (silence is no objection; an admin can also pay it straight away once the 7 days are up), re-drives `PENDING` ledger rows older than an hour (verify a deposit, re-send a transfer, re-send a refund that never reached the provider, ask after one that did), and applies the privacy policy's retention periods (a missing person's details seven days after the bounty closes, claim text a year after, never under a `LEGAL_HOLD`). Each step runs on its own: a failure in one never keeps money from moving in another.
- **The admin queue** is everything that needs a human: `PENDING_REVIEW`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION`, `RELEASING` and `FROZEN` bounties, plus any with a claim to check, a poster's objection, a report, a failed payout or a cancellation request; and, whatever its status, any bounty with a failed refund still owed or a mismatched deposit. A `MISMATCH` is resolved by hand in the Paystack dashboard and stays listed.

## State machines

```
Bounty — the happy path, then the exits

 PENDING_PAYMENT ─paid─► OPEN ─admin approves a claim─► AWAITING_VERIFICATION ─admin verifies─► RELEASING ─transfer settles─► PAID
       │ missing person   ▲                                 │ admin rejects the claim, or the claimant    │ bank reverses the payout: stays
       ▼                  │ admin approves                  │ withdraws it: back to OPEN, or EXPIRED +    │ RELEASING, claim back to VERIFIED,
  PENDING_REVIEW ─────────┘                                 │ refund if its time ran out                  │ admin retries to the current account
       │ admin declines ─► CANCELLED (refund)

 PENDING_PAYMENT ─unpaid for 24 h (sweep), or deleted by the poster─► ABANDONED (a payment landing afterwards is taken in and returned)
 OPEN ─past its date with no live claim (sweep)─► EXPIRED (refund)
 OPEN · PENDING_REVIEW ─poster cancels while no claim is live─► CANCELLED (refund)
 OPEN · AWAITING_VERIFICATION · PENDING_REVIEW · FROZEN · RELEASING after a failed payout ─admin cancels, optionally taking the page down─► CANCELLED (refund; live claims REJECTED as BOUNTY_CLOSED)
 OPEN · AWAITING_VERIFICATION · PENDING_REVIEW ─chargeback opened, or admin─► FROZEN ─admin unfreezes─► the status it came from
 FROZEN ─the cardholder wins the dispute─► CANCELLED (the bank's refund is the refund; nothing else is sent)
 AWAITING_VERIFICATION ─admin rejects the approved claim after the date─► EXPIRED (refund) if no other claim is still being checked, else OPEN for the sweep to expire later

Claim (the poster sees nothing before APPROVED)

 SUBMITTED ─admin checks and approves─► APPROVED ─poster confirms, or 7 days pass─► CONFIRMED ─admin verifies after the cooling-off─► VERIFIED ─transfer settles─► PAID
     │                                     │                                                                                          │ bank reverses ─► VERIFIED (retried)
     │                                     └─poster objects─► DISPUTED ─admin verifies anyway, recorded as ADMIN_OVERRIDE─► VERIFIED
     ├─admin rejects (before or after approval)─► REJECTED
     ├─claimant withdraws (any time before VERIFIED)─► WITHDRAWN
     └─the bounty closes─► REJECTED (BOUNTY_CLOSED) · another claim is paid─► REJECTED (OTHER_CLAIM_PAID)
```

Only one claim per bounty can be approved at a time; other claims wait as `SUBMITTED` until it is paid (they are then closed) or rejected or withdrawn (the bounty reopens and the next can be approved). `FROZEN` remembers where it came from (`frozen_from_status`). `PAID` stays on the board as "Honoured"; `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` pages still open, so a shared link explains itself, but only if the bounty was ever listed (`listed_at`) and wasn't taken down (`removed_at`).

## Providers

The bounty code imports a `PaymentProvider` interface (`apps/api/src/payments/types.ts`) and nothing provider-specific: `createCheckout` · `verifyCheckout` · `listBanks` · `createRecipient` · `transfer` · `fetchTransfer` · `refund` · `parseWebhook`, in cents, with our references echoed back. Swapping the provider is a new file behind that interface.

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **Paystack** ✅ now | ZAR card checkout (cards only, so a refund always has somewhere to go), refunds against the transaction, `basa` bank-account recipients and transfers from the balance, a `GET /bank?currency=ZAR` list. Webhooks arrive signed with HMAC-SHA512 of the raw body in `x-paystack-signature`, compared in constant time before anything is parsed. Test and live keys run the same code (`mode` is read off the `sk_live_` prefix). Needs a registered business with transfers enabled and OTP approval off. What it isn't: a licensed escrow. The money sits in CIVIC/ONE's Paystack balance, which is the legal question in the gate. |
| **TradeSafe** | A licensed South African escrow, which would take the hold leg off CIVIC/ONE and may settle the TPPP question outright, at a per-transaction fee and with its own onboarding. Not built; the interface above is the whole contract, so it can be added later without touching the bounty code. |
| **Ozow** | Instant EFT, which many posters would prefer to a card, but no refund API: a cancelled bounty would need a manual payout to a bank account, which is exactly the wallet behaviour the rules avoid. Could join later as a second checkout channel if refunds are solved. |
| **Sandbox** (local and tests only) | `sandboxProvider`: instant, deterministic, in memory. A checkout is "paid" when the return page calls `sandboxCompleteCheckout` (`SUCCESS` / `FAILED` / `WRONG_AMOUNT` exercise the settle, fail and mismatch paths); a transfer to an account ending `0000` fails and one ending `9999` succeeds then reads back as reversed; refunds settle at once; no webhook ever verifies. `readEnv` refuses it in QA and prod. |

## Configuration

- **`PAYMENTS_PROVIDER`** = `disabled` | `sandbox` | `paystack`. Locally, unset means `sandbox`; in `qa` and `prod`, unset means `disabled`, and `sandbox` is refused outright. `paystack` needs `PAYSTACK_SECRET_KEY` (inline, local) or `PAYSTACK_SECRET_ARN` (deployed). `disabled` keeps the board readable (`paymentsStatus: DISABLED`); `createBounty` and `setPayoutAccount` refuse with "Bounties are coming soon. Posting opens once payments go live."
- **The secret** is one Secrets Manager secret as JSON, `{"secretKey": "sk_…", "fingerprintKey": "<random, never rotated>"}`. The fingerprint key is separate so the Paystack key can be rotated without orphaning every payout account; deployed, a secret without one is refused. A secret that can't be read or is malformed switches bounties off for that container (`paymentsStatus: DISABLED`, an error in the logs) rather than failing every request; the deploy's smoke test runs with `EXPECT_PAYMENTS=1` wherever a secret name is set and fails on a 404 from the webhook route, so a provider that silently went missing fails the deploy. The CDK stack sets `PAYMENTS_PROVIDER=paystack` and `PAYSTACK_SECRET_ARN`, and grants the GraphQL Lambda read on the secret, only when the stage has a secret name (`PAYSTACK_SECRET_NAME` on the GitHub environment → `QA_`/`PROD_PAYSTACK_SECRET_NAME`). No secret name, no provider.
- **`ADMIN_HANDLES`**: comma-separated handles who review bounties and release money, checked by `ctx.requireAdmin()`. It is a list, not a role: sign-in never hands it out, and `viewerIsAdmin` is visible only to the account owner. Locally it defaults to `civicone`, the newsroom demo account; deployed it defaults to nobody and is set per environment. Check who owns a handle before naming it: handles come from the sign-in email's local part, and an existing member could already hold a plausible one.
- **`BOUNTY_CLAIM_MIN_ACCOUNT_AGE_HOURS`**: how old an account must be to claim; 72 when unset or empty, `0` to switch the rule off (useful locally with a brand-new sign-in; the seeded demo accounts are months old). Anything that isn't a whole number of hours stops the API at startup rather than silently switching the rule off.
- **`SITE_URL` / `PUBLIC_CONFIG_PARAM`**: where checkout sends people back to (`/bounties/<id>/paid`). Locally `SITE_URL`, defaulting to the first CORS origin. Deployed, the API reads `CIVIC_SITE_URL` from the Web stack's SSM parameter `/civic/<stage>/web/public-config` at cold start, like the SSR Lambda does; it doesn't exist on the very first deploy, when nothing can be paid for anyway.
- **The webhook route** is `POST /payments/paystack/webhook`: CloudFront `/payments/*` (no cache, all viewer headers) → HTTP API `/payments/{proxy+}` → the GraphQL Lambda, which verifies the signature over the raw bytes before reading the body. 401 for a bad signature, 404 while no provider is configured, 200 with the outcome, 500 so Paystack redelivers. The dev server mounts the same path. The direct API Gateway URL also works for the Paystack dashboard.
- **The sweep** is an EventBridge rule, every hour, invoking the same GraphQL Lambda with `{ "source": "civic.sweep" }` (an event without a `requestContext` runs `sweep()` instead of Yoga). It exists only where `payments.sweep` is true, which is prod: on QA it would keep Aurora from pausing, so QA is driven by the admin mutation `sweepBounties`, which runs the same function anywhere.
- **The smoke test** posts an unsigned body to `/payments/paystack/webhook` on every deploy and expects 401 (provider on) or 404 (disabled). Anything else means CloudFront or the HTTP API isn't wired.

## The go-live gate

Everything up to here runs on QA with test keys. Before `PAYSTACK_SECRET_NAME` is set on the `production` environment:

- [ ] **A written legal opinion** on whether this flow (Paystack checkout → CIVIC/ONE's Paystack balance → CIVIC/ONE-instructed transfer) makes the operator a third-party payment provider under NPS Act s 7 and SARB Directive 1 of 2007, and if so whether to register through a sponsoring bank or restructure so a licensed holder (TradeSafe) takes the hold leg; whether the refundable hold is a "deposit" under the Banks Act; and whether the operator is an accountable institution under FICA Schedule 1 item 19, with a proportionate RMCP for a one-person operator at a R50 000 cap.
- [ ] **Paystack's written acceptance** of the hold-and-later-payout use case for a South African merchant (its ineligible list includes remittance and wallet businesses), and confirmation that the Paystack balance can fund transfers, that is, that it is the settlement destination for ZAR card revenue, or what a top-up costs and how long it takes.
- [ ] **A registered business entity** as the Paystack merchant (transfers are for registered businesses) and as the operator named in the terms and privacy policy.
- [ ] **The secret**, created by an account administrator because the deploy role can't create secrets: `aws secretsmanager create-secret --name civic/prod/paystack --secret-string '{"secretKey":"sk_live_…","fingerprintKey":"<openssl rand -base64 32>"}'`, then `PAYSTACK_SECRET_NAME=civic/prod/paystack` on the `production` environment. `ADMIN_HANDLES` on `production` names at least one reviewer whose Google or Apple account has MFA, since there is no in-app MFA.
- [ ] **A R2 test transaction and one R1 transfer on QA** with test keys (`civic/qa/paystack`, `PAYSTACK_SECRET_NAME` on `qa`): the checkout settles through both the return page and the webhook, and the transfer settles to a real account. This confirms what is assumed, not verified: cents on initialize, transfer and refund; `trxref` on the callback; the ZAR bank list; duplicate-reference behaviour on transfers; whether processing fees come back on a refund.
- [ ] **A test refund, a test dispute and an abandoned checkout on QA.** Cancel a funded test bounty and watch `refund.processed` arrive with `transaction_reference` set to our `dep-…` reference and settle the `REFUND` row (the code matches on that reference; the sandbox can't exercise it). Raise a test dispute from the Paystack dashboard and read the `charge.dispute.resolve` payload in the dashboard's webhook log: the code treats a `resolution` containing "accepted" (`merchant-accepted`, `auto-accepted`) as lost and leaves anything else on hold for an admin; if Paystack's values differ, `applyChargeback` in `apps/api/src/data/bounties.ts` is the one place to change. Open a checkout, leave it, and confirm `GET /transaction/verify` answers `abandoned` and that the page can still be paid; then that an unknown reference answers 404 (or 400 "not found"), both of which `verifyCheckout` treats as "never seen".
- [ ] **The Paystack dashboard** for the live keys: webhook URL `https://<site>/payments/paystack/webhook`, transfers enabled, OTP approval for transfers switched off.
- [ ] Then the next deploy to prod runs with `PAYMENTS_PROVIDER=paystack`; the hourly sweep rule, which the stack only creates once a Paystack secret is set, starts moving money, and `paymentsStatus` reads `PAYSTACK_LIVE`.

## Risks and open questions

1. **TPPP status is the make-or-break question.** Holding posters' money and paying claimants looks like third-party payment provision under NPS Act s 7 and Directive 1 of 2007, with the Banks Act and FICA item 19 riding on it, and the regime is moving from PASA to the SARB. Live keys wait for the opinion; the provider interface keeps the TradeSafe route open.
2. **Balance versus settlement (unconfirmed, highest operational impact).** Transfers draw from the Paystack balance, while South African card revenue appears to settle to the merchant's bank account on T+2, with top-ups at 1%. If the balance can't be the settlement destination, every payout needs a manual top-up. The `FAILED` → `retryBountyPayout` path tolerates it, but "verify and pay" becomes "verify, top up, pay".
3. **One admin.** Dual control is the poster plus one human, so an admin account takeover is a total-loss event, bounded by the caps. MFA on the admin's identity provider, a second reviewer before the caps rise, and a digest of `CLAIM_VERIFIED` events to the owner.
4. **Missing-person review is a judgement call.** The case-number requirement does most of the work and the admin cancel is the safety valve, but a reviewer can't tell an estranged adult from a lost one. Consider launching property and information first, and a standing arrangement with a victim-support organisation for borderline approvals.
5. **Chargeback exposure after payout.** The 72-hour hold and the cooling-off don't cover a card's 120-day dispute window. After `PAID`, a chargeback is recorded (`CHARGEBACK` row) but the money has gone to the claimant; the loss is the platform's, capped per bounty. If it bites, lengthen the hold for first-time posters rather than adding identity checks.
6. **Refund cost.** Full refunds mean CIVIC/ONE absorbs Paystack's processing fee on every cancelled, expired or declined bounty (roughly 2.9% + R1, about R1 450 on R50 000) unless fees come back on refund, which is one of the unconfirmed facts. Deducting it sits near the CPA grey list; the terms choose the full refund.
7. **No phrase lists.** The only text rules are deterministic: no `@mentions`, no ID or card numbers, no street address in a missing-person bounty. Whether a bounty targets a person is decided by reports and the reviewer. An advisory suspect-description guard like the composer's privacy guard, routing to review rather than refusing, is the obvious next layer, and it will need tuning for Afrikaans and isiXhosa.
8. **Name validation.** `account_name` is what Paystack resolves for the recipient, or else what the claimant typed; the preflight flags a mismatch with the display name, but nobody has checked the name with the bank. Paystack Account Validation (a fee per call) once the account supports it.
9. **Electron checkout.** Paystack opens in the system browser and the return lands on the web app, which works but leaves the desktop window behind. Allowing `checkout.paystack.com` in the desktop shell's `isAuthUrl` would keep it in-app; a product call.
10. **Caps and clocks are guesses.** R200, R50 000, 3 open, 5 claims a day, 72 h, 7 and 14 days are all in one file and the terms import them, so a change is a deploy, not a rewrite.
11. **Paystack's dispute payload is read, not confirmed.** The `resolution` values the code acts on come from Paystack's resolve-dispute API and community reports, not from a captured webhook; the design fails safe (nothing is paid out while a dispute is open, and only an explicit "accepted" closes the bounty), and the QA step above checks the real values.
12. **No private channel between admins.** Every note an admin writes is shown to the party it concerns, and the dialogs say so; what shouldn't reach a party (a SAPS contact, a suspicion) has nowhere to go but outside the app. An admin-only note, and a second reviewer, come with multiple admins.
13. **Every claim waits on an admin.** Checking claims before the poster sees them (November 2026) closed the gap where a poster could use a claim and then refuse it, and keeps a missing person's whereabouts with CIVIC/ONE, but one admin now checks every claim. Claims are capped at five a day per claimant; a second reviewer comes before the caps rise.

## What the review changed (November 2026)

An adversarial review of the money and authorisation layers, reproduced against the API with the sandbox provider, found that: Paystack's refund and dispute events would never have matched a deposit (they carry our reference, the lookup used Paystack's id); a payment landing on a written-off checkout was dropped, and Paystack's `abandoned` was treated as final; a transfer reversed before settling left a bounty stuck in `RELEASING`; a webhook that failed to apply was answered `DUPLICATE` on redelivery; rejecting a confirmed claim after the date closed other live claims and never sent the refund; a failed refund reached nobody; a retried payout skipped the 72-hour hold; the held cap ignored unpaid bounties; the poster could see who reported them, a withdrawn claim, and admin notes written "for the record", while the claimant received the poster's note to the reviewer; and a declined or taken-down bounty became public on cancellation. Every finding above is fixed in this branch and covered by a test in `apps/api/test/api.test.ts` (the tests under "What the review of the money layer found"). The three review dimensions that never ran (the app, the copy, the infrastructure) are still owed a pass.

## Not done yet (recommended next)

- **Uploads:** photos as evidence. Claims take up to three `https` links, shown as text. When uploads come, strip EXIF and GPS and never embed them on the poster's page.
- **Email and push notifications** for bounty events. Every transition writes a `bounty_events` row with a `recipient_id`, and the in-app notifications read them; nothing leaves the app yet.
- **Paystack Account Validation** of payout accounts, so "name on the account" is checked rather than typed.
- **A platform fee.** `platform_fee_cents` and `vat_cents` exist on the bounty and are charged at zero; the deposit row already adds them to the reward, and the terms promise to show a fee before payment.
- **Multiple admins with MFA**, a private admin note, and a per-person freeze: today only bounties freeze, and a repeat offender is handled by hand.
- **A TradeSafe adapter** behind the same interface.
- **Removing a payout account** (today it can only be replaced), and **extending a bounty** before it expires.
- **Resolving a `MISMATCH` in-app.** A deposit whose amount didn't match is refunded by hand in the Paystack dashboard and stays in the admin queue.
- **Rate limits** on creating and cancelling bounties, cancellation requests and payout-account changes; today only claims are capped per day.
